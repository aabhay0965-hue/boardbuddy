Chapter 6: Manufacturing Industries

NCERT CBSE Notes (SEO Optimized + Exam Focused + Map Based)

Introduction

Manufacturing industries refer to the process of converting raw materials into finished goods on a large scale using machines, labour, and technology. They play a major role in economic development.

Importance of Manufacturing Industries

  • Helps in economic growth of a country.
  • Provides employment to millions of people.
  • Reduces dependency on agriculture.
  • Increases export earnings.
  • Supports urbanisation and infrastructure development.

Classification of Industries

1. On the Basis of Size

  • Large Scale Industries: High investment, big factories (steel, automobiles).
  • Small Scale Industries: Low investment, local production (handicrafts).

2. On the Basis of Raw Material

  • Agro-based: Cotton textile, sugar industry.
  • Mineral-based: Iron and steel industry.

3. On the Basis of Ownership

  • Public sector
  • Private sector
  • Joint sector

Location of Industries (Basic Factors)

Industries are not randomly located. They depend on several factors:

  • Availability of raw materials
  • Transport facilities
  • Power supply
  • Labour availability
  • Market access

NCERT Insight

Industries are the backbone of modern economies, and their location is influenced by both physical and human factors.

Iron and Steel Industry

The iron and steel industry is called the backbone of modern industry because it provides raw material for almost all other industries like machinery, construction, and transport.

Features

  • Heavy industry requiring large capital investment.
  • Located near raw materials like iron ore and coal.
  • Highly labour and transport dependent.

Major Iron and Steel Plants in India

  • Jamshedpur (Jharkhand)
  • Bhilai (Chhattisgarh)
  • Rourkela (Odisha)
  • Bokaro (Jharkhand)
  • Burnpur (West Bengal)

Cotton Textile Industry

India’s oldest and largest industry. It provides employment and contributes significantly to exports.

Features

  • Agro-based industry.
  • Requires humid climate.
  • Uses cotton as raw material.

Major Centres

  • Mumbai (Maharashtra)
  • Ahmedabad (Gujarat)
  • Coimbatore (Tamil Nadu)
  • Kanpur (Uttar Pradesh)
  • Indore (Madhya Pradesh)

Industrial Regions (Quick Understanding)

Mumbai–Pune Region

  • Textile and engineering industries.
  • Port-based trade advantage.

Hugli Region

  • Jute and engineering industries.
  • Located along Hugli River.

Gujarat Region

  • Cotton textile and petrochemical industries.
  • Strong transport and port network.

Bangalore–Chennai Region

  • IT, automobile, and engineering industries.
  • Highly urbanised industrial belt.

Chotanagpur Region

  • Iron and steel industries.
  • Rich in minerals and coal.

NCERT Insight

Industrial development depends on availability of resources, transport, and market demand. India’s industrial regions developed mainly near raw material sources and ports.

Chemical Industry

The chemical industry produces a wide range of products like acids, fertilizers, synthetic fibres, plastics, and dyes. It is one of the fastest-growing industries in India.

Importance

  • Supplies raw materials to many industries.
  • Supports agriculture (fertilizers, pesticides).
  • Used in textiles, pharmaceuticals, and plastics.

Fertilizer Industry

The fertilizer industry plays a crucial role in improving agricultural productivity by providing nutrients to soil.

Main Fertilizers

  • Nitrogen-based fertilizers
  • Phosphate fertilizers
  • Potash fertilizers

Major Centres

  • Gujarat
  • Maharashtra
  • Uttar Pradesh
  • Punjab

Information Technology (IT) Industry

The IT industry represents the modern face of manufacturing and services in India. It focuses on software development, data processing, and digital services.

Major IT Hubs

  • Bengaluru (India’s Silicon Valley)
  • Hyderabad
  • Pune
  • Chennai
  • Noida

Importance

  • Generates high employment.
  • Boosts exports of software services.
  • Connects India to global digital economy.

Industrial Pollution

Industries, while essential for development, also cause environmental problems such as air, water, and land pollution.

Types of Pollution

  • Air Pollution: Emission of harmful gases from factories.
  • Water Pollution: Discharge of waste into rivers.
  • Land Pollution: Dumping of industrial waste.

Environmental Problems from Industries

  • Global warming due to greenhouse gases.
  • Loss of biodiversity.
  • Soil contamination.
  • Health problems in nearby populations.

Measures to Control Industrial Pollution

  • Using cleaner fuels and technology.
  • Proper treatment of industrial waste.
  • Recycling and reuse of materials.
  • Strict government regulations.
  • Afforestation near industrial areas.

NCERT Insight

Industrial growth must be balanced with environmental protection. Sustainable development ensures progress without harming nature.

Key Terms

Term Meaning

Agro-based Industry Industries using agricultural raw materials.
Mineral-based Industry Industries using minerals as raw materials.
Industrial Region Area where many industries are concentrated.
Sustainable Development Development that meets present needs without harming future resources.

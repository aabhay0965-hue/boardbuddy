Chapter 1: Resources and Development

CBSE Board NCERT Notes (Easy + Exam Focused + SEO Optimized)

Introduction

Everything available in our environment that can be used to satisfy human needs is called a resource. But not everything becomes a resource automatically — it depends on technology, utility, and human knowledge.

What is a Resource?

A resource is anything that has utility and value and can be used to satisfy human needs.

Conditions for becoming a Resource

  • It must have utility.
  • It must be technologically accessible.
  • It must be economically feasible.
  • It must be culturally acceptable.

Classification of Resources

Resources are classified in different ways depending on origin, exhaustibility, ownership and development status.

Based on Origin

  • Biotic Resources: Derived from living organisms (plants, animals).
  • Abiotic Resources: Derived from non-living things (rocks, metals).

Based on Exhaustibility

  • Renewable Resources: Can be regenerated (solar, wind).
  • Non-Renewable Resources: Take millions of years to form (coal, petroleum).

Development of Resources

Resources are not evenly distributed. Some regions are rich in resources while others are poor. But overuse and misuse of resources create problems like environmental degradation.

Sustainable development means using resources carefully so that future generations can also use them.

Problems of Resource Development

  • Overuse of resources.
  • Inequality in distribution.
  • Environmental pollution.
  • Global warming and climate change.

NCERT Insight

Resources are a function of human activities. Without human knowledge and technology, even valuable materials remain useless.

Resource Planning in India

India has a huge variety of resources, but they are not evenly distributed. Some regions are rich in minerals and fertile land, while others are resource-poor. To use resources properly, India follows a systematic process called resource planning.

Steps of Resource Planning in India

  • Identification and inventory: Surveying and mapping resources.
  • Planning structure: Developing plans for resource use at regional and national levels.
  • Matching needs: Balancing resource development with environmental conservation.

Conservation of Resources

Resources are limited and can be exhausted if overused. Conservation means using resources carefully so they last longer and support future generations.

Why Conservation is Needed

  • To prevent depletion of natural resources.
  • To protect the environment from damage.
  • To maintain ecological balance.
  • To support sustainable development.

Ways to Conserve Resources

  • Using renewable energy sources like solar and wind.
  • Reducing waste and recycling materials.
  • Using water and electricity efficiently.
  • Protecting forests and wildlife.

Sustainable Development

Sustainable development means using resources in a way that meets present needs without harming the ability of future generations to meet their needs.

Key Features

  • Balanced use of resources.
  • Protection of environment.
  • Economic growth with environmental care.
  • Social equality in resource distribution.

Agenda 21

Agenda 21 is an international action plan adopted in 1992 at the Earth Summit in Rio de Janeiro. It focuses on sustainable development globally.

Main Objectives

  • Combat environmental degradation.
  • Promote sustainable resource use.
  • Encourage global cooperation.
  • Improve quality of life for all people.

Land as a Resource

Land is one of the most important natural resources. It supports agriculture, forestry, mining, housing and industries.

Uses of Land

  • Agriculture and farming.
  • Forests and wildlife habitats.
  • Human settlements.
  • Industrial development.

Land Degradation

Land degradation refers to the decline in the quality of land due to natural and human activities.

Causes of Land Degradation

  • Deforestation.
  • Overgrazing.
  • Mining activities.
  • Over-irrigation causing salinity.

NCERT Insight

Resource planning is essential for balanced development. Without proper planning, resources may get depleted quickly, leading to environmental and economic problems.

Land Use Pattern in India

Land use refers to how land is utilized for different purposes such as agriculture, forestry, housing, industries, and transport. In India, land use patterns vary due to physical, economic, and human factors.

Major Land Use Categories

  • Agricultural land
  • Forest land
  • Land for housing and settlements
  • Land for industries and infrastructure
  • Waste land (uncultivable or barren land)

Factors Affecting Land Use

  • Physical factors: Climate, soil type, topography.
  • Economic factors: Industrial development, population pressure.
  • Human factors: Government policies, technology, urbanisation.

Soil as a Resource

Soil is one of the most important renewable natural resources. It supports plant growth and is essential for agriculture and food production.

Factors of Soil Formation

  • Parent rock material
  • Climate conditions
  • Relief (slope and elevation)
  • Vegetation and organisms
  • Time

Major Types of Soil in India

1. Alluvial Soil

  • Found in northern plains and river valleys.
  • Very fertile and suitable for agriculture.
  • Good for crops like rice, wheat, sugarcane.

2. Black Soil

  • Also known as Regur soil.
  • Found in Deccan plateau.
  • Ideal for cotton cultivation.

3. Red and Yellow Soil

  • Formed due to weathering of igneous rocks.
  • Found in southern and eastern parts of India.
  • Suitable for millets and pulses.

4. Laterite Soil

  • Found in areas with heavy rainfall.
  • Low fertility but used with fertilizers.

5. Arid Soil

  • Found in desert regions like Rajasthan.
  • Sandy and low in moisture.

6. Forest Soil

  • Found in hilly and forested areas.
  • Varies in composition depending on region.

Soil Erosion

Soil erosion is the removal of the top fertile layer of soil by natural agents like wind and water.

Causes of Soil Erosion

  • Deforestation
  • Overgrazing
  • Heavy rainfall
  • Improper farming methods

Soil Conservation Methods

  • Afforestation: Planting trees to prevent erosion.
  • Terrace farming: Farming on steps in hilly areas.
  • Contour ploughing: Ploughing along contour lines.
  • Strip cropping: Growing crops in alternate strips.
  • Check dams: Small barriers to control water flow.

NCERT Insight

Soil is a living resource. If it is destroyed, it takes hundreds of years to form again, making soil conservation extremely important for sustainable agriculture.

Chapter 2: Forest and Wildlife Resources

CBSE Board NCERT Notes (Easy + Exam Focused + SEO Optimized)

Introduction

Forest and wildlife are an essential part of our natural environment. They maintain ecological balance, support biodiversity, and provide resources like timber, food, medicine, and livelihood to millions of people.

Importance of Forest and Wildlife

Forests and wildlife are not just natural resources; they are life-support systems for the planet.

  • Maintain ecological balance.
  • Support biodiversity.
  • Regulate climate and rainfall.
  • Prevent soil erosion and floods.
  • Provide livelihood to forest communities.

Types of Species

1. Normal Species

  • Species whose population is stable.

2. Endangered Species

  • Species that are at risk of extinction.

3. Vulnerable Species

  • Species that may become endangered in future.

4. Rare Species

  • Species with very small populations.

5. Endemic Species

  • Species found only in a particular region.

Flora and Fauna in India

India is rich in biodiversity. It has a wide variety of plants (flora) and animals (fauna) due to its diverse climate and geography.

  • India has tropical forests, deciduous forests, and alpine vegetation.
  • Many species are unique to India.
  • However, biodiversity is under threat due to human activities.

NCERT Insight

Biodiversity refers to the variety of living organisms in a particular habitat. It is essential for maintaining ecological balance on Earth.

Causes of Depletion of Forests and Wildlife

  • Deforestation for agriculture and settlements.
  • Industrial development.
  • Overexploitation of resources.
  • Illegal hunting (poaching).
  • Forest fires.

Need for Conservation

Conservation of forests and wildlife is necessary to maintain ecological stability and protect future generations.

  • To preserve biodiversity.
  • To maintain environmental balance.
  • To protect endangered species.
  • To ensure sustainable use of resources.

Conservation of Forest and Wildlife

Conservation means protecting and managing forests and wildlife so that they remain available for future generations. It is not about stopping use completely, but using them in a balanced and sustainable way.

Types of Conservation

  • In-situ conservation: Protecting species in their natural habitat.
  • Ex-situ conservation: Protecting species outside their natural habitat.

Protected Areas in India

India has created protected areas to conserve forests and wildlife. These areas restrict human activities to protect biodiversity.

Main Types of Protected Areas

  • National Parks: Fully protected areas for wildlife and ecosystems.
  • Wildlife Sanctuaries: Areas where animals are protected but limited human activity is allowed.
  • Biosphere Reserves: Large protected areas that conserve biodiversity and support sustainable use.

Important Examples of Protected Areas

  • Jim Corbett National Park (Uttarakhand)
  • Kaziranga National Park (Assam)
  • Gir National Park (Gujarat)
  • Ranthambore National Park (Rajasthan)

Community Participation in Conservation

Local communities play an important role in protecting forests and wildlife. Many traditional practices help conserve nature.

Examples of Community Efforts

  • Chipko Movement in Uttarakhand.
  • Bishnoi community protection of Khejri trees and blackbucks.
  • Van Panchayats in Himalayan regions.

Joint Forest Management (JFM)

Joint Forest Management is a system where local communities and the government work together to protect and manage forests.

Features of JFM

  • Community involvement in forest protection.
  • Sharing of forest benefits between government and villagers.
  • Helps in reducing deforestation.
  • Encourages sustainable use of resources.

Sacred Groves

Sacred groves are patches of forest preserved by local communities due to religious beliefs. Cutting trees or hunting animals is strictly prohibited in these areas.

Importance of Sacred Groves

  • Protect biodiversity naturally.
  • Preserve rare plant and animal species.
  • Maintain ecological balance.

NCERT Insight

Conservation is most effective when local communities actively participate. Forest protection is not only a government responsibility but also a social responsibility.

Distribution of Forests in India

India has diverse forest types due to variations in climate, rainfall, and altitude. These forests are not evenly distributed and change from region to region.

Main Types of Forests

  • Tropical Evergreen Forests: Dense forests found in high rainfall areas like Western Ghats and Northeast India.
  • Tropical Deciduous Forests: Most common forests in India where trees shed leaves in dry season.
  • Thorn Forests: Found in dry regions like Rajasthan and Gujarat.
  • Mountain Forests: Found in Himalayan regions with changing vegetation based on altitude.
  • Mangrove Forests: Found in coastal areas like Sundarbans, adapted to salty water.

Causes of Forest Degradation

Forest degradation means the decline in quality and quantity of forests due to human and natural activities.

Main Causes

  • Expansion of agriculture.
  • Urbanisation and industrialisation.
  • Overgrazing by livestock.
  • Illegal logging and deforestation.
  • Forest fires.

Human-Wildlife Conflict

As human population increases, forests shrink and wildlife comes into contact with humans more frequently, leading to conflicts.

Examples of Conflict

  • Elephants damaging crops in villages.
  • Tigers and leopards entering human settlements.
  • Loss of livestock due to wild animals.

Solutions

  • Creating buffer zones around forests.
  • Better forest management.
  • Awareness among local communities.

Wildlife Protection Act

The Wildlife Protection Act was introduced in 1972 to protect wild animals, birds, and plants.

Main Features

  • Prohibition of hunting endangered species.
  • Establishment of protected areas.
  • Legal protection to wildlife species.
  • Strict punishment for poaching.

Important Conservation Projects

Project Tiger

  • Launched in 1973.
  • Aim: Protect tiger population in India.
  • Creation of tiger reserves.

Project Elephant

  • Aim: Protect elephants and their habitats.
  • Focus on reducing human-elephant conflict.

Biodiversity Hotspots in India

Biodiversity hotspots are regions with high species diversity and high risk of extinction.

Indian Hotspots

  • Himalayan Region
  • Western Ghats
  • Indo-Burma Region
  • Sundaland (Nicobar Islands)

NCERT Insight

Forest conservation is not just about protecting trees. It is about protecting entire ecosystems that support life, climate balance, and human survival.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

What is biodiversity?

Biodiversity is the variety of living organisms found in a region.

What is the difference between National Park and Wildlife Sanctuary?

National Parks have stricter protection, while Wildlife Sanctuaries allow limited human activities.

What is Project Tiger?

It is a conservation program started in 1973 to protect tigers in India.

Why is forest conservation important?

It helps maintain ecological balance and protects wildlife habitats.
